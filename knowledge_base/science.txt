WATER AND TEMPERATURE
At standard atmospheric pressure, which is the pressure at sea level, water boils at 100 degrees Celsius (212 degrees Fahrenheit). Water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius at the same pressure.

The boiling point of water falls as altitude increases because atmospheric pressure decreases. On a high mountain, water can boil at around 70 degrees Celsius, which is why food takes longer to cook there.

The chemical formula of water is H2O: each molecule contains two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom.

PHYSICS
The speed of light in a vacuum is exactly 299,792,458 metres per second. Nothing carrying information can travel faster than light in a vacuum.

The acceleration due to gravity at the surface of the Earth is approximately 9.81 metres per second squared.

Sound travels at roughly 343 metres per second in dry air at 20 degrees Celsius, which is far slower than light. This is why thunder is heard after lightning is seen.

EARTH AND SPACE
The atmosphere of Earth is about 78 percent nitrogen and about 21 percent oxygen, with argon, carbon dioxide and other gases making up the remaining one percent.

The Earth orbits the Sun once every 365.25 days approximately, which is why a leap day is added every four years.

The Great Wall of China is not visible to the unaided eye from space or from the Moon. This is a widely repeated myth and it is false.

BIOLOGY
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants use sunlight, carbon dioxide and water to produce glucose and release oxygen. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts of plant cells.

The adult human body normally contains 206 bones. A newborn baby has around 270 bones, many of which fuse during growth.

Human blood is red, not blue. Deoxygenated blood in veins is a darker red, and the blue appearance of veins through the skin is an optical effect.
